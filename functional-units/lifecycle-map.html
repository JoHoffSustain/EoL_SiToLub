Power loss and wear volume

Power loss goes straight into the energy module. The reference energy demand stays fixed, and only the difference between the lubricants is added:

E = energy_spec · total + (P_loss − P_loss_ref) · hours / η

For the car the same difference becomes diesel: Δdiesel = ΔP_loss / avg_speed · 3.6 / (η_engine · 43 MJ/kg). Ask partners for both lubricants at the same operating point, ideally weighted over the duty cycle.

Wear volume is used as a ratio, so model errors in the simulation largely cancel out:

  • Filled for life: it sets the lifetime of the fill, lifetime_assessed = lifetime_lubricant · V_ref / V_assessed, and with that the lubricant consumption.
  • Optional for worn material: wear_rate = wear_rate_ref · V_assessed / V_ref. Turning wear volume into an absolute wear rate needs a wear limit, the simulated duration and a test-to-field factor, so keep that as a sensitivity.

Power loss and wear both follow the friction coefficient. If one lubricant shows less wear but more power loss, check the data with the partner.

How the threshold works

Corrosion damage is first reworked or partly replaced, so the replaced mass grows with the failure share. Once the share reaches exchange_threshold, the part is scrapped and the whole m_material_protected is replaced.

The jump makes results sensitive near the threshold: a benchmark just above it and an assessed product just below it differ by almost the whole part. Vary the threshold in a sensitivity analysis and justify its value, for example with a rust grade or a functional limit.
